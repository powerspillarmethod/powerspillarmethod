About Me

Welcome to The Powers Pillar Method I'm Wes, and I built this because one way isn't the only way

Over the past six years I have spent countless hours researching, reading, and testing (most of it on myself). What I found is that the people who truly transform their health aren't the ones who found the perfect diet. They're the ones who finally understood the full picture.

The Powers Pillar Method was created from that understanding: one pillar is never enough. How you sleep, how you move, and who you surround yourself with are just as powerful as what you eat. When all four pillars are strong, everything changes.

As a certified nutrition coach, I work with clients who are done with short-term fixes and ready to build a life that feels genuinely, sustainably healthy.

Questions

Everything you want to know

How is this different from other nutrition coaching? +
Most nutrition coaching focuses exclusively on food. The Powers Pillar Method addresses all four systems: nutrition, lifestyle, sleep, and relationships — because they're all interconnected. Improving one without addressing the others only gets you so far.
How long does the program last? +
The core program is 12 weeks, which gives us enough time to move through all four pillars and then integrate them. Many clients continue after 12 weeks either with ongoing monthly support or by joining a group coaching cohort.
Do I need to follow a specific diet? +
No restrictive diets, no calorie counting unless you want it. The Powers Pillar Method is rooted in whole-food, anti-inflammatory eating and a healthy relationship with food not deprivation or rigid rules.
Where do sessions take place? +
All coaching sessions are held via Zoom, so you can work with me from anywhere in the world. Sessions are typically 45 minutes and scheduled at a time that works for your timezone.
What if I've already tried everything? +
That's exactly who I work best with. If you've tried nutrition programs, fitness plans, or wellness apps and nothing has stuck it's likely because you were only addressing one pillar. The four-pillar approach reveals the gaps that have been holding you back.
Is there a payment plan? +
Yes. All packages are billed monthly so there's no large upfront commitment. I also offer flexible arrangements for longer commitments — ask about this on your discovery call.

The Science Behind the Method

Evidence-based coaching across all four pillars

The Powers Pillar Method is not built on trends or opinions. Every pillar is grounded in peer-reviewed research, meta-analyses, and clinical studies. Here is the science we coach from.

🥗 Nutrition · The BMJ, 2019

Ultra-Processed Food & Cardiovascular Disease Risk

Srour et al. · Prospective cohort study · 105,159 participants

A 10% increase in ultra-processed food consumption was associated with significant increases in cardiovascular disease risk, independent of nutritional quality. Whole food foundations aren't about perfection — they directly reduce the inflammatory processes driving chronic disease.

→ Applied in Session 2: Whole Food Foundations
🥗 Nutrition · AJCN, 2015

Protein Intake & Appetite Regulation

Leidy et al. · Systematic review

Higher protein intake (25–30% of calories) significantly increased satiety hormones and reduced hunger hormones, leading to lower total calorie intake without deliberate restriction. Protein at every meal is the most evidence-backed way to naturally reduce hunger without willpower.

→ Applied in Session 3: Macros & Gut Health
🥗 Nutrition · Nature, 2006

Gut Microbiota & Metabolic Health

Turnbaugh et al. · Landmark microbiome study

Gut microbiota composition significantly influences energy harvest, fat storage, and metabolic rate. Dietary fiber was the primary driver of microbiome diversity. Your gut bacteria are influencing your weight, cravings, and mood — and food is how you talk to them.

→ Applied in Session 3: Gut-Brain Connection
🌿 Lifestyle · Journal of Psychosomatic Research, 2002

Chronic Stress & the HPA Axis

Tsigos & Chrousos · Review of HPA axis research

Chronic psychological stress activates the HPA axis, producing sustained cortisol elevation that leads to visceral fat deposition, insulin resistance, immune suppression, and sleep disruption. Stress isn't just a feeling — it's a hormone that physically changes where your body stores fat.

→ Applied in Session 4: Stress Physiology
🌿 Lifestyle · Frontiers in Psychiatry, 2015

Exercise as a Stress Buffer

Zschucke et al. · Systematic review of 15 RCTs

Regular aerobic exercise significantly reduced cortisol reactivity to psychological stressors and improved HPA axis regulation. Even moderate activity (30 min, 3–5 days/week) produced measurable neuroendocrine benefits within 4–6 weeks. Exercise rewires your stress response system.

→ Applied in Session 4: Movement as Medicine
🌿 Lifestyle · PNAS, 2015

Blue Light & Melatonin Suppression

Chang et al. · Randomized crossover trial

Evening blue light exposure suppressed melatonin onset by approximately 1.5 hours and reduced REM sleep quality. Your phone before bed isn't just a distraction — it chemically delays your sleep hormone by up to 90 minutes. That's biology, not a discipline issue.

→ Applied in Session 5: Evening Rituals
🌙 Sleep · Annals of Internal Medicine, 2004

Sleep Deprivation & Hunger Hormones

Spiegel et al. · RCT, 12 healthy participants

Two nights of sleep restriction reduced the satiety hormone leptin by 18% and increased the hunger hormone ghrelin by 28%, resulting in a 24% increase in appetite with specific cravings for high-carbohydrate foods. Poor sleep literally changes your hunger biology — it's not a lack of willpower.

→ Applied in Session 6: Sleep & Nutrition Connection
🌙 Sleep · PNAS, 2009

Circadian Misalignment & Metabolic Disease

Scheer et al. · Controlled circadian misalignment protocol

Irregular sleep timing independently increased postprandial glucose, insulin, blood pressure, and cortisol — changes similar to pre-diabetes — separate from total sleep duration. Consistent sleep and wake times are as important as how many hours you sleep.

→ Applied in Session 6: Circadian Rhythm
🌙 Sleep · Annals of Internal Medicine, 2015

CBT-I: The Gold Standard for Insomnia

Trauer et al. · Meta-analysis of 20 RCTs

Cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia significantly improved sleep onset, total sleep time, and sleep efficiency with effects maintained at 3–12 month follow-up — outperforming sleep medication. The most effective treatment for chronic sleep problems is changing thoughts and behaviors around sleep.

→ Applied in Session 7: Sleep Anxiety & Racing Mind
🤝 Relationships · NEJM, 2007

Social Networks & the Spread of Obesity

Christakis & Fowler · Longitudinal study · 12,067 participants

A person's risk of becoming obese increased by 57% if a close friend became obese. Health behaviors spread through social networks via shared norms. The people you spend the most time with are shaping your health outcomes whether you realize it or not.

→ Applied in Session 8: Social Environment
🤝 Relationships · Perspectives on Psychological Science, 2015

Loneliness as a Mortality Risk Factor

Holt-Lunstad et al. · Meta-analysis · 308,849 participants

Social isolation increased mortality risk comparably to smoking 15 cigarettes per day — exceeding the mortality risk of obesity and physical inactivity. Loneliness is not just an emotional state — it elevates inflammation markers at the same level as chronic smoking.

→ Applied in Session 8: Belonging & Connection
🤝 Relationships · Journal of Social & Clinical Psychology, 2007

Self-Compassion & Emotional Eating

Adams & Leary · Experimental study

Participants who practiced self-compassion after eating a "forbidden food" consumed significantly less at subsequent meals compared to self-critical participants. Self-criticism triggers shame spirals and further emotional eating. Self-compassion interrupts the cycle — it is the evidence-based strategy, not indulgence.

→ Applied in Session 9: Self-Relationship
✦ Integration · European Journal of Social Psychology, 2010

How Long Habits Actually Take to Form

Lally et al. · Longitudinal study · 96 participants

New habits took an average of 66 days to become automatic — far longer than the popular "21-day" myth. Consistency of context was the strongest predictor of habit formation. Missing one occasion did not negatively affect long-term formation. You haven't failed if it still feels like effort at Week 10.

→ Applied in Session 10: Integration & Non-Negotiables
✦ Integration · American Psychologist, 1999

Habit Stacking & Implementation Intentions

Gollwitzer · Review of 94 studies

Implementation intentions — "When X happens I will do Y" plans — increased goal achievement rates by an average of 300% compared to simple goal setting. Linking new behaviors to existing habits leverages existing neurological pathways rather than building new ones from scratch.

→ Applied in Session 11: Habit Stacking
✦ Integration · British Journal of Sports Medicine, 2021

The Four Pillars as One Integrated System

Walsh et al. · Systematic review

Sleep, physical activity, nutrition, and social connection function as an integrated biological system rather than independent lifestyle factors. Improvement in any one domain produced measurable improvements in the others through shared pathways including cortisol regulation, inflammation, and insulin sensitivity.

→ Applied in Session 10: Why the 4-Pillar Approach Works

All studies referenced are peer-reviewed and published in accredited scientific journals. References available upon request.
